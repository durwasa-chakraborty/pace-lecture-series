#### **Talk**
- **Topic:** Datatypes in, Zippers out!

- **Description:** A talk on our paper which has been submitted to PLDI 2027. 


Zippers let a program focus on one position inside a data structure, edit it, and keep moving without re-searching from the root. They’re common in editors walking syntax trees and in consumers of lazily produced data. Datatype derivatives tell you the type of a zipper’s context, but not how to represent it concretely or what each move costs.

This talk presents Kamot, a tool that turns algebraic datatype declarations into zipper implementations with operational contracts. The generated moves are proved correct against a reference traversal. They come with sound bounds on work and on how much of the input gets forced, and they are guaranteed to terminate even on infinite (coinductive) inputs. The analysis is also complete: when no certificate is issued, some input makes the move diverge. Key results are verified in Lean 4. Across 19 datatype models and 81 generated families, all generated code type-checks and passes independent validation. A prefetching case study shows where these static bounds beat dynamic search and where they fall short.
